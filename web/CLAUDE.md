# web/ guidelines

Loaded when working in `web/`. These are the single source of truth for the UI rules; the root CLAUDE.md only points here.

- **Thin view layer.** Components render a `PlayerView` and emit player intents. No game logic in components. The renderer must be swappable: PixiJS draws the iso view, with the old DOM renderer still selectable (`ROOM_RENDERER` in `web/src/rendererChoice.ts`). Keep chat and text input in the DOM regardless of renderer. I want to experiment with custom animated UI later, so do not couple rendering to game logic.

- **Mobile is first-class.** Below 1024px wide (`MOBILE_QUERY` in `web/src/useMobile.ts`, mirrored by one `@media` block in `styles/responsive.css`) the game is a tabbed phone layout: the top bar (one strip: clock, alerts, menu) always shows; one panel at a time below it; a bottom nav of Inspect (left) | Room (middle, default) | iTrackie (right), as tall as the top bar. Your ID card (`hud/PlayerSummary.tsx`) is over the room view's top-left corner, as the desktop's is. Tapping a person, equipment, or a door in the room fills the peek bar, the bottom of the room view's panel with a divider over it (legal actions, Go for doors, More to open Inspect; the selection pulses in the room instead of being named). A minimap of the whole ship (`scene/Minimap.tsx`) sits in the room view's bottom-left corner on both layouts (hover a room, or tap it on touch, to see its name and who is in it); doors are still the main way to move. Every UI change must work in both layouts:
  - Nothing important may be hover-only or depend on the `dblclick` event (touch has neither reliably).
  - Every button that does an action or a move takes a double press, so a stray click never spends points: `ActionButton`, or `useDoublePress` / `DoublePressButton` (`hud/DoublePress.tsx`) for others. It counts `click` events, so two taps and two Enter presses work too; the first press arms the button (`data-armed`, drawn pressed with a white outline). Selecting, walking and opening panels stay single-click; a confirm dialog's Yes does too. Actions too inconsequential to need it (free, undone by closing a window; so far only `access`, opening a terminal) are listed in `SINGLE_PRESS_ACTIONS` there and take one press; add to it only with the owner's say-so. The inventory panel's Take/Drop button on a selected tile also takes one press, always: selecting the tile is the first press. A per-device "single press for actions" setting (`pressMode.ts`) makes every one of them fire on the first press; without it, a player with five or fewer double-press actions done whose presses keep lapsing gets the `doublePress` tip (in the tip card) after three lapsed presses in a row, until an action fires; "Show all tips again" restarts its count.
  - The iso room always draws at exactly 1x (one screen pixel per source pixel, never scaled up or down); a room bigger than its panel is never panned by hand: the camera follows your character (click or tap floor to walk; acting on equipment steps you up to it), always centred on you, even near the room edges.
  - 360px wide is the minimum supported width, and must look good, not just work. No sideways page scroll; nothing under the notch or the nav.
  - Panels are hidden with CSS, not unmounted, so chat drafts and tabs survive switching.
  - When the user asks for a visual check (`play` skill, never run unprompted), check both screenshot passes. The on-screen keyboard can only be checked on a real phone.

- **Buttons use the Mush style.** Every button uses the global `button` rule in `styles/buttons.css`: the original site's 3-slice sprites with hover and pressed variants, or `button.big` for the 40px variant. New buttons set only layout (size, padding, grid), never their own background, border, or colors. Base UI has no theme object; its parts render native elements, so a Base UI part that renders a `<button>` picks up the same rule, and its states are styled through the `data-*` attributes it sets. The only deliberate exceptions are widgets that aren't plain buttons (tabs), which replace the background.
- **Unavailable actions show disabled, not hidden** (owner's preference): when an action can't be done right now (used up today, on cooldown, short of AP, full hands, a broken part), show its button disabled with the reason, unless the owner says otherwise. The rules make that possible: `validate` returns one of the reasons in `UNAFFORDABLE_REASONS` (`shared/src/rules.ts`), so the option reaches `view.actions` with `lacks`, and `DisabledActionButton` (`hud/ActionButton.tsx`) shows why. Hide an action only when it doesn't apply to the situation at all, e.g. no Leave orbit while the ship isn't orbiting a planet, or an action for a title or skill you don't have.
- **Modals use the terminal frame.** It is the owner's preferred modal style: the iTrackie's dark glowing `kit-panel` around a light cyan `cut-card`, with a deeper cyan title bar (`DialogHeader` with `terminal-header`, title left, × right), a scrolling body, and the gold outline drawn by `TabOutline`. The styles are in `hud/terminal.css` and `hud/settings.css`. Build one with `FramedDialog` (`hud/FramedDialog.tsx`) inside your own `Dialog.Root`; its children are the body, and a body that is one `.settings-tabs` Tabs.Root gets tabs on the tab line (`hud/SettingsDialog.tsx`). Terminals use `TerminalFrame` instead. Content inside the frame sits on a light card, so it uses the dark text tokens `cut-card` sets, not the dark-panel colours. Controls take their look from the surface they sit on: text fields read the `--field-*` tokens (dark in `tokens.css`, white with the ink edge in `cut-card.css`) through one zero-specificity rule in `base.css`, so a new field needs no colours of its own; add tokens the same way rather than overriding per dialog. A bare `.settings-popup` (navy) and the other one-off popup styles are older; the owner moves those to the frame one at a time, so don't convert them unasked. `ConfirmPanel` keeps the original game's pop-up art.
- **Collapsibles on terminals use `TerminalCollapsible`.** Build collapsible captions or sections inside a terminal with `TerminalCollapsible` (`hud/TerminalCollapsible.tsx`, styles in `hud/terminal.css`), which wraps Base UI's `Collapsible`. They use the terminal header background (`--terminal-header-bg`) with a `1px solid var(--terminal-rule)` border and `Mush/www/img/icons/ui/less.png` (open) / `more.png` (closed) as the toggle icon (`COLLAPSE_ICON` / `EXPAND_ICON` in `art/assets.ts`), replacing the global Mush button sprite on the trigger. Default reference/caption text to closed (`defaultOpen={false}`).
- **Shared primitives before one-off styles.** `styles/primitives.css` holds the small recurring patterns: `.field` with `.field-label` (a bold label above its control, on a `label` or a `fieldset`), `.pick-tile` (a portrait/name tile toggled with `aria-pressed`; the user sets only its width) and `.sub` (a muted note). Each reads surface tokens, so it is right on the dark panels and on the light card; a new one adds tokens to `tokens.css` and `cut-card.css` like `--pick-*`. The design kit's "Primitives on both surfaces" section (`designKit/PrimitivesSection.tsx`) shows each on both surfaces; add new ones there. Add a primitive only when a second user needs it.
- **Never use the `title` attribute.** It is invisible on touch and to keyboard users, and screen readers handle it inconsistently. Name an element with visible text or `aria-label`. Extra explanation goes in a Base UI `Tooltip` (a label, as `IconButton` does) or `Popover` (a card with a description, as `SkillSlots` and `StatusIcons` do), opened on hover and focus; the popover also opens on tap. `Hint` (in `IconButton.tsx`) adds a tooltip to an existing button. `tools/tests/a11y/noTitleAttr.test.ts` fails on any `title=` in the client, so a component prop must not be called `title` either.

- **No scrollbars in the isometric view.** The iso panel never shows a scrollbar (it is `overflow: hidden`; the camera scrolls it programmatically to follow you). Art is never scaled: crisp, unaliased pixels only.

- **Render cost.** Every new view re-renders the whole game screen, and every panel stays mounted (hidden with CSS), so a component's cost is paid on every move, in every tab, for every copy. In dev, `RenderWatch` (`src/RenderWatch.tsx`) warns in the console when one update passes 100ms (`SLOW_RENDER_MS`); treat that warning as a bug. Rules that keep updates cheap:
  - **Unchanged parts of the view keep their identity.** `GameScreen` passes each incoming view through `shareUnchanged` (`src/shareView.ts`), so `view.chat`, `view.players` and the rest are the same objects as last time unless they changed. That is what lets `memo` skip; never bypass it when storing a new view.
  - **Memoize heavy and repeated components on what they read.** A component that takes the whole `view` but reads a few parts gets a comparator on those parts by reference (`sameThread` in `hud/Thread.tsx`). List rows get primitive props and compare their item with `jsonEqual` (`ChatRow` there, `LogRow` in `hud/ITrackie.tsx`), since a list matched by position loses sharing when an item drops from its front. Callbacks to memoized components must be stable, or be read through a ref (`useHandlers` in `Thread.tsx`); JSX props can't be compared, so pass a data key beside them (`headKey`).
  - **Work derived from static data happens once,** at module level or in a module cache, never per render (`spotsFor` in `scene/Minimap.tsx` caches room layouts from the ship plan).
  - **Pass narrow props.** A component given the whole `view` re-renders on every update; one given `view.ship` can be memoized. Prefer narrow props for anything heavy.
  - **Mind multiplication.** Something cheap inside a row is paid per row, per mounted channel or thread (a popover per message, an input per thread).
  - **Test with a full game:** full chat pages, many threads, 16 players. Small games hide costs that grow with volume.
