# Rules design: decisions and phased plan (written 2026-10-05, pruned 2026-10-13)

> **Bots (2026-10-13):** the author does not necessarily plan to run AI-controlled bots in live public games. They may be for testing only. Wherever these docs describe bots filling seats, treat live use as undecided.

The record of answered rule decisions and the design for the rules that are not built yet. Open questions live in GitHub issues labeled `rule-decision`, not here. Scheduled plans have their own document, `docs/action-plans.md`.

**Status (2026-10-13):** built are AP, MP, HP and morale values, the action registry, inventory, the iTrackie, global chat, and private channels. Not built: infection, spores, the attack, win conditions, the cycle timer, and the phases below.

## Decisions received

- **Timed actions** (2026-10-17, owner): every action takes 2 seconds of real time and happens when its bar runs out ("at the end", with `config.action.resolveAt: "start"` kept as the alternative: happen at once, then a cooldown). Moving takes 2 seconds too. A player can queue up to 3 actions behind the one under way and has a Clear queue button, which also cancels the one under way. The cost is checked again and paid when the action happens, so a dropped or cancelled action costs nothing; one that is no longer possible when its turn comes is dropped with a private log line. End cycle (`ready`) is instant; actions under way carry over into the next cycle, and nothing new can be queued once ready. A bar shows over the actor's head to people in the room when the action's log entry could reach them: public and secret actions always, covert ones only on camera, private ones never (owner: "any action that results in a room log"). The bar never says which action. My defaults: `access` is untimed too (it only opens the terminal window); `pick_skill` is timed.
- **Mush-eating bacteria research** (2026-10-17, owner): once complete, a human needs 4 contamination to convert instead of 3, for every human. Humans already at 3 are not converted when it completes (my default).
- **Caffeinomaniac** (2026-10-16, owner): consuming a coffee gives +2 AP on top of the coffee's own, capped at the AP cap; coffee only; the NERON project bonus is not built.
- **Terminals** (2026-10-16, owner): every terminal gets an `access` action that opens a blank window showing its name; the terminals' real actions come later. Free and unrestricted for now (my default; the original has a `MODULING` status and access limits for Command and BIOS, not built). Terminals: Astro, Command, Com center, NERON core, BIOS, Research lab, Pilgred, Calculator.
- **Myco alarms** (2026-10-17, owner): completing the MYCO_ALARM research yields three alarms, left on the lab floor (my default for placement, confirmed). They ring (public room log, no actor, no `danger` level yet) when a Mush uses: Mutate, Punching, Infectious cuddles, Fungal kitchen, Trapper, Transfer, Nightmarish, Bacterophiliac or Radio pirate; spiking also counts (#159). Spore removal is not detected. Only active in a room inventory, never carried. Dismantle uses the standard dismantle config (confirmed). Only spiking is wired; the skills are not built.
- **Research** (2026-10-16, owner): working on a research costs 2 AP and advances it 25%; no repeat penalty for now; anyone not dirty can contribute; every research that has card art is in (24); the list and progress are fully public. My defaults, not confirmed: it is done at the lab's research terminal, a finished research cannot be worked on, any number can be in progress at once, and nothing happens on completion yet (the original's difficulty, samples, prerequisites and effects are ignored).
- **Blueprints** (2026-10-16, owner): building always consumes the blueprint; no failure chance; anyone can build anywhere; ingredients may be in your inventory or on the room floor. Build costs 3 AP (the original's value). A built sofa is room equipment, not an item. The sofa needs metal scraps + a thick tube (the original's recipe). For now only a sofa blueprint exists, in the front corridor (CORRIDOR_A). Rocket launcher blueprint (2026-10-16, owner): thick tube + metal + plastic scraps (the original's first recipe), builds an effect-less item into the inventory or onto the floor, spawns in the front corridor. Open: other recipes, where blueprints come from beyond this one, drawing built equipment.

- **Schrödinger the cat** (2026-10-16, owner; story starts in **private beta**, nothing is built yet):
  - **What he is:** an item that can be carried (inventory slot like any item, or on the room floor). Item icon: `Mush/www/img/icons/items/body_cat.jpg` (`ui/cat.png` is something else, not his). Original id `BODY_CAT`, described as "a bit grumpy but entirely silky".
  - **Infection mechanic (to be built):** a Mush who has the cat in their own inventory can use *infectious caress* (original `MUSH_CARESS`, Mush only, 1 AP, tracked) to make him a Mush cat. A human who then takes him up is contaminated (the original log: "X was contaminated by Y by taking Schrödinger. Their contamination level is now N."). It feeds the same contamination and conversion rules as `spike`.
  - **Death (to be built):** his death has consequences for Raluca's morale. Only this fact is recorded; the amount, the cause of death, and the announcements are not specified.
  - **No win condition.** The original's three Eden objectives about him are dropped.
  - **From the original, not decided here** (`Mush/derived/data/DataAction/actions.json`): a normal `CARESS` (1 AP, unique bonus of +3 morale per the original footnote) and `PICK_CAT` (0 AP) exist; he also wanders, stows away on expeditions, and has NERON quips. None of these are decisions yet.
  - **Open (ask before building):** whether taking an infected cat always contaminates and by how much; whether it happens once or every time; whether he can be infected more than once or cured; who sees the contamination log line (the victim, Mush, or the room); whether the cat's Mush state is hidden from humans in `viewFor` (it must be); the exact morale effect and Raluca's relation to him; whether he moves on his own.
- **Item attributes and Heavy** (2026-10-16, owner): items can have attributes that work the same on every item that has them (`ItemType`); the first is `heavy`. Carrying any heavy item makes each room-to-room move cost 2 more MP (`heavyMpPenalty`), once however many you carry; floor items do not count. The `sturdy` skill (Solid, "Robuste") cancels it and adds 1 to punch damage (`sturdyPunchBonus`); Throw Out a crewmate is not built. The freezer is heavy and spawns in the refectory at game start. The Inspect panel tags heavy items.
- **Skill picking** (2026-10-15, owner): all 58 human skills are pickable by everyone for now (each character will get its own set later). Picks are free, permanent, and need a free slot (3; the book slot is separate and later). Nobody starts with Creative any more. Skills are public to anyone in the same room as the player, and show only on the Inspect portrait (not the self panel); only your own portrait has the plus button. Most skills do nothing yet. Descriptions are not shown (they would be rules text). Supersedes the Creative-at-start and "not shown to anyone else" parts of the next entry.
- **Skills and Creative** (2026-10-15, owner): every player has 3 human skill slots; a fourth comes only from books (later). Players will be able to pick skills at any time through a UI (later). For now every player spawns with Creative in one slot and the other two empty. Creative: each failure of an action that cost AP has a fixed 50% chance to give back 1 AP (the retained `CREATIVE` row in `skills.json`; the older 25% `CREATIF` row is ignored). It can't exceed the 12 AP cap because the AP was just spent; no action costs MP. A refund writes a private log line. Skills are not shown to anyone else until there is a skills UI.
- **ACTOPI and the minimap** (2026-10-13): everyone's location stays visible on the minimap, but who is where is only known while the ship's ACTOPI system is on. With it off you know who is in your own room, and everyone else is an unnamed dot (`mush.png`). ACTOPI is a ship-wide in-game system state that starts on. What turns it off or on is **undecided**; for now it is only a flag (`GameState.actopi`) with nothing switching it.
- **Players:** 16 seats, 2 Mush. If 8 or fewer players are in the room when Mush are selected, only 1 Mush. Selection may happen later than game start (for example on a later day). The code still has the placeholder of 8 seats.
- **Joining and Mush selection** (2026-10-13): one plain queue per language. Joining puts a player into that language's open ship, which is in a pre-selection phase where no one is Mush. The game starts, and Mush are chosen, when the ship fills (16) or on day 3 cycle 8, whichever comes first, with whoever has joined. There is no minimum player count: 8 or fewer players get 1 Mush, more get 2. During pre-selection there are no spore actions (there are no Mush) and all combat is disabled. Multiplayer has no bots; bots exist only in single player.
- **Single player** (2026-10-13): 9 seats. The human is always the only Mush, against 8 bots who are all human. This overrides the player-count rule (9 players would otherwise get 2 Mush). Cycles advance when the player ends them, not on the timer. Stage not decided.
- **Cycles:** AP refills on a timer, 3 hours per cycle and 8 per day (timer decided 2026-10-05, length 2026-10-13). There is no way to end a cycle early, so the "ready" action goes away. AP: start 8, +1 per cycle (+2 lying in a bed), cap 12, unspent AP carries over (2026-10-11).
- **Days** (2026-10-14): a day is 8 cycles. When it ends, every living player loses 1 morale (floor 0, nothing else happens at 0 yet). This applies from day 1, including pre-selection. The UI shows "Day N · Cycle M" (cycle within the day) and the log says "Day N begins." The log was added to the existing global cycle counter without changing it.
- **Bonding, DTT, and STT** (2026-10-14): `bond` costs 1 AP and is permanent (same for Mush). It is one-way, so each of two characters bonds with their own AP, and bonds can be made with as many people as you like. All three actions need the other person in your room. `dtt` and `stt` cost 1 AP, paid by only one of the two, and need a bond in both directions (the bond is the consent). Each gives both +2 morale (cap 14) and writes a plain private log line for each of the two. A pair shares one cooldown: after either action, both partners are blocked from DTT and STT with anyone until the next day begins (no repeats, never both on one day). A bond is visible to its two partners only (`PlayerView.bonds`, shown as a tag in Inspect). A `dtt` with exactly one Mush raises the human's contamination by 1; STT, human-human, and Mush-Mush do nothing. Default contamination capacity is 3 (raisable by modifiers via `contaminationCapacity`). Reaching it will convert the human once infection exists. The counter is hidden from everyone, including its owner. What the acronyms stand for is deliberately not written anywhere.
- **Replays** (2026-10-14; to be done by the end of private beta): every game is recorded, and its replay becomes viewable only after the game ends. A replay shows everything: roles, every room, hidden stats, and private channels. Finished games are publicly listed in chronological order. A per-player "my games" list waits for accounts. Moderators also see the raw chat text before moderation changed or blocked it. They do not see the rate-limited messages themselves, only how many messages each player had rejected by the rate limit, so they aren't wading through spam. Implementation notes: the game's setup (seats, config, seed, rules version) plus an append-only log of every input is the source of truth, and the rules re-run it, so every state change goes through one input reducer.
- **Healing** (2026-10-15, owner): the `heal` action needs a `medikit` in your own inventory (reusable, not consumed) and a living target in your room, or yourself. Costs 2 AP on someone else, 3 AP on yourself. Restores 3 HP by default (modifier hook `healAmount`; skills and research will raise it), capped at max HP. It is offered even at full HP, and writes a public room-history line. Anyone can heal; no medic role is needed. Heal uses the existing medikit, which spawns in SICK_BAY (owner confirmed this is the one they meant).
- **Nurse skill** (2026-10-15, owner): the nurse (`first_aid`) grants 2 care points at the start of every day and 2 more the moment it is picked.
- **Medic skill, care points, sick bay healing** (2026-10-15, owner): the nurse skill (not medic; corrected later the same day) grants 2 care points at the start of every day (and at game start), added to the old unused `heal` resource counter. Unused points accumulate with no cap (open: add a cap?). While you hold one, any heal (self or other) costs 0 AP and spends one automatically; they do not replace the means to heal. In the SICK_BAY anyone can heal without a medikit at the normal AP costs. 
- **Punch** (2026-10-15, owner; issue 29): the `punch` action costs 1 AP and deals 2 damage (`hit` elsewhere in this document). Only a living person in your own room can be targeted, never yourself. It misses 40% of the time (the AP is still spent). It is a public action: hit or miss, a line goes in the room's history. HP stops at 0 (death is undecided); no per-cycle limit was set. Cameras are not considered yet.
- **Statuses and satiety** (2026-10-14, owner): the first statuses are `dirty` (cheese icon, public), `full belly` (apple icon, private) and `hungry` (fork icon, private). Extracting a spore always makes you dirty; dirty has no removal until a cleaning action exists. Humans (not Mush) have satiety: start 0, no cap, most consumables raise it, it falls 1 per cycle (some things will make it fall more; modifier hook `satietyPerCycle`), never below 0. At 3 or more satiety you have full belly and can't eat anything. After exactly 24 cycles following the cycle satiety reached 0 (the game start counts), if still 0, you are hungry and lose 1 HP per cycle; any consumable resets the count and ends hunger. Mush have no satiety, never get hungry and can eat freely. Placeholders: coffee +1 satiety, `cooked_ration` +4. HP stops at 0 (death is undecided). Public statuses show on the Inspect panel for people in your room; private ones only in your own view.
- **Cooking** (2026-10-16, owner): an `uncooked_ration` in the refectory (carried or on the floor) can be cooked for 1 AP into a `cooked_ration`, which replaces it in place. Only in the refectory, no equipment needed, public room log. Uncooked rations cannot be eaten. The three starting refectory rations are now uncooked.
- **Showering** (2026-10-14, owner): the `shower` action targets a shower (Bedroom Alpha and Beta have one) and is only offered while dirty. It costs 2 AP, 1 less if soap is in your inventory (the soap stays). It removes `dirty`; a Mush also takes 4 damage (HP stops at 0). The log line is private: nobody sees a shower, only the soap being taken and dropped in public history. One soap always spawns in the Alpha dorm for now, with the `soap` item icon.
- **Spores:** Mush have a spore resource. `extract_spore` costs 1 AP, gives 1 spore, and always applies the **dirty** status. Only a fixed number of spores can be extracted per day.
  - **Extraction answers (2026-10-14, owner):** Mush only. Each Mush can extract once per 4 cycles (a cooldown on the global cycle counter, not tied to days; ready immediately when Mush are chosen). Each Mush holds their own spore count (no per-Mush limit for now). The whole Mush faction shares a daily cap, 4 by default, reset at the day boundary; skills and research will modify it (modifier hook `sporesPerDay`). At the cap the action is not offered. Teammates' spore counts will be visible to Mush later; no UI place yet. The log line is private (changed to secret 2026-10-17, owner: room history if witnessed or filmed). **Dirty is deferred** until a status system exists (done 2026-10-14, see "Statuses and satiety").
  - **Spiking (2026-10-15, owner):** `spike` targets a living human in your room (not a Mush). Mush only; costs 1 AP and 1 spore; always succeeds; raises the human's contamination by 1, capped at capacity (3 by default, `contaminationCapacity`). The victim is not told. The log line is secret: other living players in the room (never the victim) see it, otherwise only the Mush does. (Superseded 2026-10-17: spiking is covert, see "Covert actions and cameras".) Contamination stays hidden from every view.
  - **Conversion (2026-10-15, owner):** a human whose contamination reaches capacity (from a spike or a DTT) becomes Mush at once, in the same action. They are told only by a private log line, which does not say who caused it. Human skills stay, and Mush can keep learning human skills like anyone else. The convert can extract a spore straight away. No effect on HP or morale. No cure for the MVP.
  - **Contamination status (2026-10-16, owner, to help debug infection):** Mush can see a human's contamination level as a status effect. The human can never see it on themself, and no human sees it on anyone. Like other statuses, it shows only for people in your room (Claude's default, not stated by the owner).
- **Death** (2026-10-16, owner; #28, #45): reaching 0 HP from any source (punch, hunger, a Mush's shower, anything later) kills at once, in the same action or tick. 0 morale does nothing yet (to be designed later). Death is permanent; nothing can ever revive anyone. On death: the body becomes a `corpse` item in the room's inventory (a room holding a corpse draws that character lying on the floor); the iTrackie is destroyed; every other item drops into the room. Everyone is told by a crew-wide NERON message that names the dead player and reveals their role (human or Mush); until NERON exists it is a system message in global chat. The death message does not give the cause yet (a public beta story). The room where the death happened always gets a public log line, whether or not anyone is there, since the global message may later be switchable off. A corpse can be picked up and carried like any item and takes one inventory slot; it is heavy (owner, 2026-10-16); while it is in someone's inventory, the body is no longer drawn on the floor. `jettison` destroys a carried corpse in one of the four bays (Alpha Bay, Alpha Bay 2, Bravo Bay, Icarus Bay): 0 AP, a secret action (covert 2026-10-17, secret again 2026-10-18). The dead player leaves all their private channels, and each gets a system message that they have left this world and the chat. The dead player's bonds and contamination are voided, and plans targeting them are cancelled (their own claims and locked AP are already released by death, see `docs/action-plans.md`). A dead player learns nothing new (no roles, no hidden state). A dead player can read, never post in, global chat and the feature channels (no iTrackie needed); they talk in the **graveyard** instead (owner, 2026-10-17), a chat channel only the dead read or post in, with no iTrackie needed and its history visible to anyone who dies later; a dead Mush can also read the Mush channel. A dead player sees nothing that depends on being in a room: no room log, no other players' locations, no room inventory.
  - **Ghosts** (later, public beta): a dead player walks the ship as a ghost with infinite MP, still seeing nothing room-based. A ghost may leave and join another ship. Until ghosts exist, a dead player may leave and join another ship too.
- **Combat:** no voting. The basic attack (the punch, `hit` in this document) costs 1 AP and deals 2 damage (changed from 1 on 2026-10-13; a placeholder until combat is designed). Max HP 14, start 14; morale max 14, start 7; both private to their owner (2026-10-11).
- **Win conditions:**
  - Mush win if all humans are dead, or if the ship returns to Sol while any Mush is alive.
  - Humans win if the ship returns to Sol with no Mush alive.
- **Visibility:** Mush have a private chat channel. Nobody ever sees another player's faction, Mush included (owner, 2026-10-16): a faction indicator shows only on a player's own summary, and Mush learn who the other Mush are only through the Mush channel. Spiking items (e.g. coffee) are descoped from #25.
- **Chat:** room speech is always available and is written into the room log like a public action. Global chat requires an **iTrackie** in your inventory.
- **Inventory** (2026-10-11): 3 private slots, unlimited room storage, free take/drop logged publicly.
- **iTrackie** (2026-10-13): everyone starts with one; it is a normal item (uses a slot, can be dropped or taken); carrying one is required to read and post in global chat and the feature threads.
- **Room log visibility** (2026-10-11): the room log is that room's history. Private actions never show to other players; secret actions are logged to the room only if another player is there to witness them (this will change). Implemented in `shared/src/log.ts` (`logPublic`/`logPrivate`/`logSecret`/`logCovert`).
- **Covert actions and cameras** (2026-10-17, owner; #36, #35): a fourth visibility, covert: bystanders do not see it; it becomes room history only if a camera is in the room, otherwise only the actor sees it. Secret actions are also revealed by a camera (room history even with no witness). A camera's reveal is ordinary room history (anyone in the room then or later) and shows everyone, including someone a secret line normally hides from (a spike's victim). Until camera installation (#35) exists, a camera means a `camera` item in the room's inventory. Spiking is covert (it was secret). Jettisoning was covert too, but is secret again (owner, 2026-10-18). Extracting a spore is secret (it was private; owner, 2026-10-17); STT and DTT stay private. How a failure is logged is decided per action, with no general rule (owner, 2026-10-17): a missed punch is public, a failed dismantle private. Action buttons show the visibility as an icon after the label: padlock (private), hushing hand (secret), camera (covert); public actions have none. **Cameras as equipment (2026-10-17, owner; #35):** an installed camera is `CAMERA` room equipment (one per room); a `camera` item, carried or lying in a room, is not part of the room's equipment and does not film. `install` costs 1 AP, needs no tool, uses up a carried camera item and adds the room's camera equipment; logged publicly. `uninstall` removes the camera from the room's equipment and gives you a `camera` item (into the room if you have no space); its cost is a **placeholder** (1 AP, `uninstall.cost.ap`), and it is logged publicly (Claude's default, not stated). `dismantle` also works on an installed camera: it removes it from the room's equipment and leaves a metal scrap instead of a camera item, with the usual dismantle cost and odds (3 AP, 12%); a camera item that is not installed can be dismantled the same way. Open: real uninstall costs and tools, who sees uninstalling, and whether a camera item can be picked up by anyone.
- **Titles and succession** (2026-10-14, #144): each title has its own line of succession, an ordered list of the crew (the order for each title is **undecided**). Titles are issued at the same time as Mush selection, each to the first person in its line. When a holder dies or goes inactive, the title passes to the next person in the line who is alive and active. A former holder who becomes active again does not get the title back; they can only regain it through the normal succession, if the current holder loses it and they are the next eligible person. Which titles exist is **undecided**; the communications officer is one.
- **Trading crew to merchant ships** (2026-10-14, #143): the communications officer can trade crew members away to a merchant ship, but only **inactive** players (#145). Trading away an inactive Mush is allowed and removes them from the ship. Inactive and super inactive are two levels of status (Private beta); their thresholds are **undecided**.

## Phases

Each phase ends with typecheck and tests passing and a small commit. Phase headings note which open questions (now `rule-decision` issues) they wait on.

### Phase 1: rules core in `shared` (needs the infection, Sol, and death decisions)

- **Time as an input, not a clock read.** Add a `tick` that the server calls when a cycle boundary passes, for example `advanceCycles(state, n)`. The rules stay pure and tests can jump days instantly.
  - Tradeoff: the server owns real time and the rules only count cycles. That keeps rules deterministic, at the cost of the server having to compute how many cycles were missed after downtime.
- **Delete `ready`.** Cycles advance only on `tick`.
- **Player state additions:** HP, status effects (starting with `dirty`), spore count, and an inventory (minimal, only what the iTrackie needs).
- **Ship state:** spores extracted today (reset at each day boundary), the ship's position or Sol-return progress once the Sol trip is decided, and whether Mush have been selected yet.
- **Mush selection:** a separate step (`selectMush`) run when the selection trigger is decided. It picks 1 or 2 Mush from the eligible players using the seeded RNG.
- **Actions:**
  - `extract_spore`: enforces the daily cap.
  - The infect action, once decided.
  - `hit`: decrements HP; at 0 HP the player dies.
  - The return-to-Sol action, once decided.
- **Win/end checks** after every action and tick. When the game ends, `phase: "ended"` records the winner.
- **Tests:**
  - Mush count is 1 vs 2 around the 8-player boundary.
  - The daily spore cap holds and resets on the day boundary.
  - Extracting a spore always applies `dirty`.
  - AP refills only on a tick, never by action.
  - Each win condition triggers correctly.
  - Determinism for a given seed.

### Phase 2: visibility and chat rules in `shared` (mostly built: global and private channels)

- **`viewFor`:**
  - A Mush sees the roles of the other Mush; everyone else still sees no roles.
  - Log entries get a scope: public, room, Mush-only, or private.
  - Tests check, for every seat, that a human's view has no roles except their own.
- **`say` action:** writes a room-scoped log entry and costs no AP (I'll assume no cost unless you say otherwise).
- **Global chat:** a separate message type allowed only for players holding an iTrackie.
- **Mush chat:** a channel filtered in `viewFor`, the same as the log scopes.

### Phase 3: server (needs the late-joiner and bot-seat decisions)

- **`GameRoom` timing:** use a Durable Object alarm for cycle ticks instead of `setTimeout`, so ticks survive restarts. On wake, apply any missed cycles.
- **Dev time scale:** an env var (for example `CYCLE_SECONDS=30` locally) so a "day" passes in minutes during testing.
- **Matchmaker:** 16 seats, plus the late-join behavior once decided.
- **Chat safety:**
  - A per-player chat rate limit.
  - A max message length.
  - A basic word-filter moderation hook, plain code with no new dependency to start.
  - A report/mute path deferred and noted.
- **Fix the known gap:** idle humans can no longer stall the game, because ticks ignore readiness.


### Phase 4: client

- Show HP, status effects, spores (Mush only), and the cycle countdown.
- A Mush chat tab, shown only to Mush.
- Still plain DOM, still driven only by `PlayerView`.

### Phase 5: local model bots

- **Recommendation:** run a local OpenAI-compatible server, either **Ollama** (easiest) or **llama.cpp's `llama-server`** (lighter, more control), with a ~4B instruct model at Q4 quantization.
  - On 8 GB VRAM that leaves room for long prompts.
  - An ~8B Q4 model also fits but is tight.
  - Check current model rankings when picking a specific model.
- **Bot interface:**
  - The model-backed bot speaks the OpenAI chat-completions format, with base URL, model name, and key in `.dev.vars`.
  - Switching later to a cheap hosted API, or to Triton (which also offers an OpenAI-compatible frontend), is then a config change, not a code change.
  - When you want a cheap hosted fallback, compare current per-token prices; I have no live pricing.
- **Fits the cycle structure:** cycles last hours, so bots can queue requests one at a time and the single GPU is never a bottleneck.
- **Safeguards:**
  - The bot must pick from `view.legalActions`. Any invalid output falls back to the scripted bot.
  - Player chat goes into the prompt marked as untrusted quoted text.
  - A per-game request cap.
  - The "hard spend cap" becomes a token/request budget, kept so it still works if you switch to a hosted API.
- **Dependency note:** Ollama or llama.cpp is a system install, not an npm package. I'll ask before installing either.
- **Public access later:** running everything on your machine is fine for development. When the game goes public, either deploy the game server to Cloudflare and keep only the model local, or expose your machine through a tunnel. Decide this at deploy time.

- **Medic skill** (2026-10-15, owner): heal actions restore 2 more HP (`medicHealBonus`). It will also do surgery, medicine reading and research bonuses; not built yet.
- **Mush channel** (2026-10-16, owner): a `mush` chat channel that only living Mush read and post, with no iTrackie needed. Whenever a human's contamination rises, the game posts a system message there with the human's name and new level (never the Mush responsible); reaching capacity posts a conversion message with the name. A convert gets the full history. Ways for humans to gain access come later. **Only one private Mush channel will ever exist** (owner, 2026-10-16; settles #39).
- **Antique Perfume** (2026-10-16, owner): each shower makes you immune to infection for 2 cycles.
